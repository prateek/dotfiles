# Discover regional sources

Read when the task concerns an unfamiliar region or local ecosystem, or the seed
catalogue misses a relevant audience. Apply the multilingual workflow first.

1. Establish the evidence jurisdiction or buying market and languages that matter.
   Use existing context or ask only when an unknown changes feasibility. Finish
   with the relevant locality; do not infer it from the user's nationality.
2. Find a local primary source, specialist community and independent alternative
   when useful. Search native phrases for the topic plus terms meaning forum,
   owner review, repair, university surplus or public auction. Follow local
   citations and community links. Finish with candidate sources and why each fits.
3. Check source ownership, audience, current activity, topical section and access.
   An English-language expat forum may miss local users; a national site may miss
   city-level stock or pickup. Finish with qualified scope or explicit uncertainty.
4. Use available routes and inspect original evidence. Broaden to another language
   or source family when it adds a missing perspective, within the same budget.

Illustrative seeds to evaluate, not a region-wide coverage claim:

| Branch | Possible leads and complementary evidence |
| --- | --- |
| South/Southeast Asia | Pantip, Kaskus, local technical forums and public community groups; check local-language manufacturer or retailer evidence |
| Africa | Nairaland or relevant country/city forums; local publications, official portals and specialist communities rather than a single continental default |
| Latin America | Local forums, Spanish/Portuguese developer communities and public groups; Mercado Libre or country-specific commerce evidence when buying |
| Europe | Local technical forums, Habr when relevant to Russian-language engineering, public institutions and regional resale sites; verify market/jurisdiction separately |
| Middle East | Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Turkish or other relevant language communities; official institutions and local marketplaces suited to the actual locality |
| Diaspora or cross-border audiences | Relevant diaspora communities plus evidence from both destination and origin markets; avoid treating either as universally representative |

Public Discord/Telegram archives, newsletters, podcasts, federated communities
and specialist mailing lists may hold valuable evidence wherever their audience
matches. Search only accessible material; private memberships and closed groups
are access gaps unless an authorized session supplies the needed content.

Finish with source-selection reasons and actual coverage. Add a reusable regional
profile only after recurring use demonstrates useful decision rules; one query
does not require another skill or a permanent catalogue entry.
